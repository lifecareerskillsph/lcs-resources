Cooperation, Competition, Conflict, and Accommodation

Part B of Understanding the Nature and Dynamics of Social Relationships. This is not graded. In Facilitated mode, notes appear before group activities; in Self-Paced mode, work through independently.

Learn

Four social processes

Part B looks at four modes of relating (Park & Burgess, 1921). They are not fixed stages that every relationship must pass through.

ProcessMain patternQuestion to recognize it
CooperationWorking toward shared success.How do we achieve this together?
CompetitionStriving for a limited reward or position.Who performs best under the rules?
ConflictClashing goals, needs, values, or actions.What seems incompatible?
AccommodationA workable arrangement reduces open conflict.What can we live with for now?

Sources: Park & Burgess (1921); Deutsch (1973).

Learn

Five elements of cooperation

Johnson and Johnson's model helps distinguish actual cooperation from simply dividing a document (Johnson & Johnson, 2009).

ElementEvidence in a group task
Positive interdependenceThe work needs every member's contribution.
Individual accountabilityEach person can explain their part and the whole project.
Promotive interactionMembers explain, encourage, and give useful feedback.
Social skillsMembers listen, manage disagreement, and repair mistakes.
Group processingThe group checks what worked and what needs to change.

Social loafing and shared effort

People may reduce effort when individual contributions disappear into a pooled group result. What helps: visible roles, clear individual deliverables, and brief check-ins. Before labeling someone, remember that a missing contribution does not prove laziness. Ask about obstacles and clarify expectations.

Bayanihan highlights shared effort and responsibility. Ask whether every member has a way to contribute.

Practice

Practice 5: elements of cooperation

A. A group output requires each member's different contribution. Which element does this demonstrate?
B. Each member must explain their contribution and understand the whole project.
C. Members submit separate sections, but nobody discusses the quality of their teamwork. Which element could help them improve together?
D. Members stop to listen when two disagree, then repair a misunderstanding about who does what.
E. Members explain their reasoning to each other and give feedback on drafts.
See the answer key

A. D: the task connects their outcomes: success needs everyone.

B. B: each member takes responsibility for a contribution and can explain the work.

C. A: members review how the group worked, what helped, and what needs to change. Simply combining sections does not establish effective cooperation.

D. C: listening and repair are social skills.

E. B: explaining and encouraging each other is promotive interaction.

Competition can help or harm

AreaHealthy patternUnhealthy pattern
FocusImprovement and doing your best.Undermining others or tying worth to winning.
RelationshipsRivals retain respect.Sabotage, withholding, or humiliation.
RulesClear and fair.Rules change to favor a winner.
AfterwardCongratulate, learn, and move on.Resentment and put-downs.
A limited prize creates competition. The way people pursue it determines its effect on the relationship.

Conflict and the way it unfolds

Conflict is a perceived incompatibility of goals, needs, values, or actions. A disagreement can reveal something that needs to change.

TypeWhat it looks likeExample
Constructive conflictPeople describe the issue, listen, keep respect, and look for a workable response.Two people want to present. They compare strengths and share roles.
Destructive conflictPersonal attacks, threats, escalating blame, and humiliation make repair harder.The same two people accuse each other of being selfish.

Practice

Practice 7: conflict styles

A. Two officers discuss what each needs and develop a plan that addresses both sets of important concerns.
B. Two officers each give up part of a demand to reach an agreement quickly.
C. A student always gives in to avoid any argument, even when the issue matters to them.
D. A group takes a short break because tempers are rising and the issue can wait.

E. How do collaborating and compromising differ? Is one conflict style best for every situation?

See the answer key

A. D: the officers seek a solution that addresses both sides' important interests. This usually requires discussion.

B. C: both sides accept partial concessions.

C. A: low concern for own interests, high for the other's. Repeated yielding can leave your needs unmet.

D. B: avoiding can help during a cooling-off period.

E. Collaboration seeks to address both sets of concerns. Compromise involves concessions. The appropriate style depends on the situation, time, and safety.

Learn

Accommodation has three meanings

The same word appears in three places. Keep them apart.
WhereMeaning
Social process in this lessonOpposing parties reach an arrangement that reduces open conflict, even if the underlying disagreement remains. A temporary peace between groups is one example.
Thomas-Kilmann conflict styleOne person yields to the other person. This is also called accommodating.
Piaget's concept from Lesson 1A person changes a mental schema to fit new information.

Forms of social accommodation

FormMeaningExample
CompromiseBoth sides give up part of a demand.Meet online on weekdays and in person on Saturday.
TolerationCoexist despite disagreement.Classmates respect differing beliefs without arguing.
TruceTemporarily stop hostilities.Two cliques stop hostile posts during exams.

Third parties in accommodation

FormThird party's roleWho shapes the outcome?
ConciliationEncourages contact and communication.The parties work toward agreement themselves.
MediationFacilitates a discussion.The parties craft their agreement.
ArbitrationHears both sides and decides.The arbitrator makes the binding decision under the process.
A workable peace should preserve safety and voice. One side always giving in can leave unfairness in place.

In the Philippines, barangay conciliation under the Katarungang Pambarangay (RA 7160) is a community example of third-party settlement.

Practice 8: forms of accommodation

A. A counselor helps two students discuss a disagreement. The students develop their own agreement.
B. A competition panel hears both sides and makes the decision under the event's rules.
C. Two groups agree to stop hostile posts during exams, although the disagreement remains.
D. Each side gives up part of its demand: meetings online on weekdays, in person on Saturday.
E. Which statement about the word "accommodation" is correct?
See the answer key

A. C: the counselor facilitates the conversation. The parties shape their agreement.

B. A: the panel hears the sides and decides. The decision is binding under the agreed process.

C. B: a temporary pause stops hostilities. It can reduce immediate harm while deeper issues still need attention.

D. D: both sides give up part of a demand.

E. C: the three meanings are distinct, as the table shows.

Conflict, bullying, and safety

What it looks likeAppropriate response
Ordinary disagreementBoth parties can express their needs and have meaningful ability to respond.Support can help them address the issue.
Bullying patternRepeated targeted harm and a power imbalance make it difficult for the target to stop what is happening. Peer negotiation alone is not enough.Tell a trusted adult and use the school's reporting or referral system. Protection of the person experiencing harm matters.
Do not wait for a pattern to be proven. Threats, intimidation, or a serious incident deserve adult attention. Philippine law, the Anti-Bullying Act (RA 10627), requires schools to address bullying.

Recap

Part B: what to remember

  • Cooperation, competition, conflict, and accommodation are modes of relating, not stages.
  • Real cooperation needs interdependence, accountability, promotive interaction, social skills, and group processing.
  • Conflict can be constructive or destructive. No conflict style is best for every situation.
  • Accommodation has three meanings. A workable peace should preserve safety and voice.
  • A bullying pattern needs adult intervention. It is not a conflict to resolve alone.

Looking ahead: Part C (lesson-5c) widens the lens to relationships across generations and across groups.
