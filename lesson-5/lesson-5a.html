Learn

Why healthy relationships matter

Three reasons recur in the research, and each needs a careful reading.

Well-being. A meta-analysis of 148 studies found that people with stronger social relationships had a substantially greater likelihood of survival over the follow-up period (Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010). This is an association. It does not establish that relationships alone cause these outcomes.

Close support. The quality of close friendships matters. A large circle or high popularity does not automatically provide reliable support.

Learning and working together. Well-structured cooperation can support achievement, positive relationships, and psychological adjustment (Johnson & Johnson, 2009).

Careful phrasing: write "is associated with", not "causes", unless the evidence supports causation.

Sources: Holt-Lunstad, Smith, & Layton (2010); Johnson & Johnson (2009).

Healthy relationship patterns

Healthy relationships are recognized by repeated patterns over time, not by one good or bad day. This checklist is a guide for reflection, not a diagnosis.

TraitWhat you would notice
RespectDifferences in opinions, pace, and boundaries receive consideration.
TrustPeople keep promises and protect private information.
Honest communicationYou can express thoughts and feelings without fear.
FairnessGiving, taking, and decisions are reasonably balanced over time.

Boundaries and safety

TraitHealthy patternWarning sign
BoundariesA refusal receives respect.Pressure, guilt, or punishment for refusing.
GrowthFriends support your study, rest, and other friendships.Isolation or jealousy used to limit your choices.
DisagreementPeople address the problem and attempt repair.Humiliation, threats, or repeated personal attacks.
SafetyYou feel physically and emotionally safe.Intimidation, control, or violence.
A relationship can have disagreements and still be healthy. Threats, control, or repeated humiliation deserve serious attention.

A next step: speak with a trusted parent or guardian, teacher, adviser, or school counselor. You can use the school's guidance and referral services (UST: the Center for Counseling and Career). When there is immediate danger: tell a trusted adult immediately and seek emergency assistance. You do not have to handle a threatening situation alone. The NCMH Crisis Hotline is 1553 (Smart/TNT: 0919-057-1553).

Social influence

Working definition: other people's real or imagined presence can change what we think, feel, or do. Influence is not always bad.

ExampleThe question to ask
Helpful influenceA classmate's study routine encourages you to prepare earlier.Does this serve my goals?
Harmful influenceA group's silence makes it harder to challenge a mocking post.Would I choose this if the group were not watching?

Reasons for following others

MotiveWhat you are seekingExample
Normative influenceAcceptance or protection from rejection.Joining a plan because you fear being left out.
Informational influenceA better answer or a guide in uncertainty.Following classmates who seem to know the correct procedure.

The same behavior can involve more than one motive. Consider the person's reason (Deutsch & Gerard, 1955).

Forms of social influence

FormKey featureExample
ConformityMatching a group without a direct request.Changing an answer after seeing everyone else's answer.
ComplianceAgreeing to an explicit request.Lending a notebook after a classmate asks.
ObedienceFollowing a direct order from an authority.Following an instruction from a teacher in the laboratory.
Quick distinction: is it a group pattern, an explicit request, or an authority's order?

Learn

Depth, classic studies, and peers

Kelman: how deep does influence go?

LevelMeaning
CompliancePublic agreement to gain a reward or avoid punishment, without necessarily accepting the belief privately.
IdentificationAdopting behavior because you admire someone or want to belong to the group.
InternalizationAccepting a belief because it fits your own values.

Terminology caution: Kelman's use of "compliance" emphasizes public agreement without private acceptance. The form-of-influence table above uses the request-based definition. Keep the two uses apart.

Classic studies and their lessons

Asch and conformity. Participants sometimes agreed with an obviously wrong group answer about line lengths. One person giving the correct answer sharply reduced conformity. An ally can make dissent easier (Asch, 1956).

Milgram and obedience. In one study condition, 26 of 40 participants continued to the maximum level on a simulated shock device. Authority and situation mattered. Results varied across conditions, and participants showed doubt and distress (Milgram, 1963).

Social loafing. Latane and colleagues found lower effort when individual contributions were pooled together. Identifiable contributions matter in group work (Latane et al., 1979).

Peers and social media

Peer presence can make some choices, including risks, more attractive during adolescence (Gardner & Steinberg, 2005). Context affects what feels easy. It does not remove your capacity to choose. Likes and shares can become social cues: a popular post can feel more credible or desirable without offering better evidence.

A response you can practice: notice the pull, pause, check your reasons, and find an ally when you need one.

Sources: Kelman (1958); Asch (1956); Milgram (1963); Latane, Williams, & Harkins (1979); Gardner & Steinberg (2005).

Social perception and attribution

Social perception is forming impressions and explaining others' behavior. Attribution is explaining behavior through the person or the situation.

ConceptMeaningExample
AttributionExplaining behavior through the person or situation.Late because of irresponsibility, or because transport failed.
Fundamental attribution errorOverweighting personality and overlooking circumstances.Calling a quiet groupmate uninterested without asking why.
Actor-observer differenceExplaining our own behavior by circumstances and others' by traits.My missed deadline is Wi-Fi trouble; yours is laziness.

Other perception shortcuts

ConceptWhat happensA caution
Thin-slice judgmentA brief observation produces an impression.Some judgments can be useful, but error remains possible.
Halo effectOne positive trait shapes unrelated judgments.Popularity does not establish honesty or skill.
Self-fulfilling prophecyAn expectation shapes behavior that helps produce the expected outcome.Expectations can matter, but outcomes are not guaranteed.
Social comparisonYou evaluate yourself against others.Online highlights leave out much of a person's life.

Sources: Heider (1958); Ross (1977); Jones & Nisbett (1972); Ambady & Rosenthal (1992); Thorndike (1920); Merton (1948); Festinger (1954).

C. You failed to submit on time and blame the Wi-Fi. When a classmate does the same, you say he is careless.

B. C: a positive impression in one area influences unrelated judgments.

C. A: same event, different attribution depending on whether it is you or the other person.

Practice

Pause A2: the silent groupmate

Scenario. A groupmate has not replied for two days. You think, "He is lazy and does not care."
  1. What attribution are you making? Name two possible situational explanations.
  2. Write a first message that checks the situation and still addresses the deadline.
See feedback

The attribution: "lazy" is a dispositional explanation. Assuming it without checking circumstances may involve fundamental attribution error.

Possible explanations: a family emergency or loss of internet could explain silence. These are possibilities to check, not facts to assume.

Possible message: "We haven't heard from you, and our deadline is Friday. Are you okay? Can you update us today on what you can finish or what support you need?"

The habit: check the situation while keeping expectations clear.

Social interaction and Filipino concepts

Working definition: interaction is an ongoing exchange of actions and responses. Norms, roles, and cultural meanings shape that exchange.

Reciprocity. People tend to return what they receive. Kindness can invite warmth, while sarcasm can invite retaliation. Your response helps shape the next interaction, and you can choose a different response without accepting mistreatment.

Social exchange. Support, companionship, effort, and strain affect how people experience relationships over time. Persistent unfairness can feel draining.

Enriquez's levels of interaction

Ibang-tao refers to relating to someone as an outsider. Hindi ibang-tao refers to treating the other as one of us. These terms describe interaction, not a compulsory ladder (Enriquez, 1992).

Ibang-tao levelPlain meaningHindi ibang-tao levelPlain meaning
PakikitungoCivility, courtesy, and polite transaction.Pakikipagpalagayang-loobMutual trust, acceptance, and feeling at ease.
PakikisalamuhaMixing with others and being in their company.PakikisangkotInvolvement in the other person's concerns.
PakikilahokJoining and participating in an activity.PakikiisaDeep oneness, trust, and a shared cause.
PakikibagayAdjusting to a group's ways.
PakikisamaGetting along and maintaining harmony.

Filipino concepts as assets and tensions

ConceptAn assetA possible tension
PakikiramdamSensitivity to others' feelings.Guessing instead of asking.
PakikisamaBelonging and goodwill.Pressure to agree against your values.
Utang na loobGratitude and mutual care.A favor used to demand compliance.
HiyaTact and concern for propriety.Reluctance to disagree or seek help.
PakikipagkapwaRecognizing shared humanity.Boundaries can blur if there is no room to say no.
Individuals differ. These are lenses for understanding a situation, not descriptions of every Filipino.

Sources: Enriquez (1992); Pe-Pua & Protacio-Marcelino (2000).

Part 1. Match the level. Choose the Enriquez level each situation shows.

Part 2. Concepts at work.

A. You agree to a group plan you dislike because you do not want to disrupt harmony. Which concept is most relevant?
B. Someone says you must do their work because they once helped you. Which concept is being used to create pressure?

C. A classmate wants to borrow your laptop for the weekend. You need it for a paper. She helped you before, and refusing feels embarrassing. Write a reply that acknowledges the favor and states a clear boundary.

See the answer key

Part 1: 1 C, 2 A, 3 B, 4 D, 5 C. Pakikitungo is civil transaction, pakikilahok is joining an activity, pakikibagay is adjusting to a group, pakikipagpalagayang-loob is mutual trust and ease, pakikiisa is deep oneness with a shared cause.

A. D: pakikisama supports harmony and belonging. Here the wish to get along creates pressure to agree.

B. A: gratitude can support mutual care. Using a past favor to demand compliance can make the relationship unfair.

C. Possible reply: "Thank you again for helping me before. I need my laptop for my paper this weekend, so I can't lend it." Check yourself: which part expresses appreciation, and which part makes the boundary clear? Hiya can make refusal uncomfortable, and utang na loob can create gratitude, but a past favor does not settle every future request.

Recap

Part A: what to remember

  • Healthy relationships are recognized by repeated patterns of respect, trust, honest communication, fairness, boundaries, support for growth, repair after disagreement, and safety.
  • Conformity matches a group, compliance follows a request, obedience follows an authority. Normative influence seeks acceptance, informational influence seeks guidance.
  • Check attributions before labeling someone. Fundamental attribution error and the halo effect are shortcuts, not facts.
  • Filipino concepts such as pakikisama and utang na loob can support relationships or create pressure. Use them as lenses.
