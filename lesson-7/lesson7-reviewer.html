Interpersonal effectiveness: three goals in almost every interaction

GoalQuestion to ask
Objective effectivenessDid I get what I needed?
Relationship effectivenessIs the relationship in good shape after this?
Self-respect effectivenessCan I respect myself for how I handled it?

Sometimes the goals conflict. The skill is weighing them on purpose (Linehan, 2015). This is a practical checklist, not the whole definition: the course also frames it through pakikipagkapwa, respect for dignity, and service.

Part A, know: effective communication

Verbal: clear and respectful

  • One cannot not communicate. Even silence sends a message (Watzlawick et al., 1967).
  • Clear messaging: lead with the point, be specific, know your listener, make one request at a time, check understanding.
  • Face and politeness. Requests, criticism, and refusals can threaten a person's "face," so speakers soften them (Brown & Levinson, 1987). This lines up with amor propio and hiya.
  • Direct and indirect. Philippine communication is often described as leaning indirect (pahiwatig, pakikiramdam), which can protect harmony but can also cause missed messages. It is a generalization, and individuals vary (Enriquez, 1992; Pe-Pua & Protacio-Marcelino, 2000). Aim for direct but warm.
  • Making a request (Linehan, 2015): DEAR MAN (Describe, Express, Assert, Reinforce, stay Mindful, Appear confident, Negotiate). Add GIVE for the relationship (Gentle, Interested, Validate, Easy manner) and FAST for self-respect (Fair, no Apologies, Stick to values, Truthful).
  • Negotiating: prepare, open respectfully, listen first, offer options, agree and confirm. Negotiating with elders is not defiance. A respectful request with reasons, and acceptance of a firm decision, shows maturity.

Nonverbal: clues, not facts

  • Channels: face and eyes, gesture and posture, touch and ritual (mano po), space, voice, time, silence, digital cues.
  • Myth check: "93% of communication is nonverbal" misreads Mehrabian and Wiener (1967). They tested single words in different tones. When words and nonverbal signals conflict, people often trust the nonverbal signal.
  • Cues are hypotheses. Display rules differ (Ekman & Friesen, 1969), and facial expressions do not map reliably onto specific emotions across people and contexts (Barrett et al., 2019). Use the loop: Observe, Interpret, Check, Adjust.
  • Digital tone is fragile. Senders overestimate how well tone such as sarcasm comes across in email (Kruger et al., 2005). Add context and check before replying in anger.
  • Sensitivity to needs: notice who is left out, tired, or struggling to follow, and adjust the pace or format.

Pause A: the cold reply

Scenario. You need to ask your homeroom adviser for permission to miss a Saturday activity because of a family obligation. Last time you sent a one-line message ("Ma'am I can't come") and she replied coldly.
  1. What went wrong in the message?
  2. Rewrite it so it is clear, respectful, and specific.
  3. Which of Linehan's three goals matter most here?

Try it: which message would you send?

Short and unexplained. It gives no reason, no respect markers, and no plan, so it can sound cold.

Respectful greeting, a clear request, a reason, and a plan. It protects the objective and the relationship.

This announces a decision rather than asking, so it can read as dismissive and risks the relationship.

See a possible response

What went wrong: no greeting, no reason, no request, and no plan. It can read as a refusal to engage.

Rewrite: "Good afternoon, Ma'am. May I be excused from Saturday's activity because of a family obligation? I will get the notes from a classmate and submit any task by Monday."

Goals: objective (permission) and relationship (trust with the adviser) matter most, with self-respect in being honest about the reason.

Part B, know: empathy, pro-social behavior, and pakikipagkapwa

Empathy and its neighbors

TermMeaningKey difference
Cognitive empathyUnderstanding what another thinks and how they see the situation.Perspective-taking.
Affective empathyFeeling something in response to the other's feeling.Feeling with.
Empathic concernWarmth and concern, wanting to help.Leads toward help.
Personal distressFeeling overwhelmed and self-focused.Can lead to withdrawal.
SympathyFeeling sorry for someone.Feeling for, from a distance.
CompassionNoticing suffering, concern, and being moved to help.Adds motivation to act.

Empathic distress can lead to withdrawal and burnout, while compassion leads to approach and help (Singer & Klimecki, 2014). Empathy has limits: it tends to be stronger for in-group members (Cikara et al., 2011), and it is not agreement. Some scholars argue empathy alone is a poor moral guide (Bloom, 2016), a debated position, and most agree it works best with fairness and compassion.

Pro-social behavior and the bystander problem

Pro-social behavior is voluntary behavior intended to benefit others (Eisenberg et al., 2006). People are less likely to help in an emergency when others are present because of diffusion of responsibility. A meta-analysis found the effect is real but weaker in clearly dangerous situations (Fischer et al., 2011). Helping takes five steps, and a break at any one stops it.

StepBarrierWhat helps
1. NoticeDistraction, phone.Look up.
2. Interpret as a problem"Nobody else seems worried."Check: "Are you okay?"
3. Take responsibility"Someone else will do it."Decide you will act, or point to a specific person.
4. Know how to helpNot knowing what to do.Learn basic steps: call for help, report, comfort.
5. Act despite the costFear, embarrassment, hiya.Start small. Safety first.

Other-centeredness and servant leadership

Other-centeredness is healthy with boundaries and a stable sense of self, and unhealthy when it becomes self-erasure. Greenleaf (1977) described the servant leader as one who leads by serving first. Servant leadership involves empowering others, humility, authenticity, standing back, and stewardship (van Dierendonck, 2011).

Pakikipagkapwa, amor propio, and hiya

PakikipagkapwaPakikisama
Core ideaShared humanity and dignity.Getting along; keeping harmony.
Saying no?Yes. Respect can include honest disagreement.Can make saying no feel costly.
RiskBlurred boundaries if no room for self.Conformity or going along with harm.

Amor propio is self-esteem and sensitivity to being slighted. Hiya is a sense of propriety or shame about how one appears (Bulatao, 1964). It can encourage tact, and it can stop people from asking for help or admitting mistakes. Undue hiya, such as shaming a classmate in front of others, is what the learning outcome warns against. Practice: correct in private, give a way to save face, be honest and kind together (I-messages).

Restorative humility

Humility is an accurate view of yourself, open to others' views (Davis et al., 2011). A useful apology acknowledges what happened, takes responsibility, expresses regret, and offers amends (Lazare, 2004). School restorative programs need good implementation, and results from a large US trial were mixed (Augustine et al., 2018), so keep claims modest.

Pause B: the screenshot

Scenario. In a group chat, your friend Ben posts a screenshot of a classmate's embarrassing mistake, and several people laugh. You reacted too. The classmate has stopped replying.
  1. Which concepts apply?
  2. Write what you would say privately to the classmate.
  3. What would you say to Ben, protecting his dignity but being honest?

Try it: what do you send the classmate?

This dismisses the harm and invites more hiya. It is not an apology.

It acknowledges, takes responsibility, expresses regret, and offers amends.

Silence leaves the classmate isolated and avoids repair.

See a possible response

Concepts: amor propio, hiya, the bystander effect (you reacted rather than stepping in), and restorative humility.

To the classmate: "I laughed at that post, and I should not have. I am sorry it embarrassed you. Can I help take it down?"

To Ben (private, honest, face-saving): "I think that post hurt her, and I regret laughing. Can we take it down together and say sorry?"

Part C, know: compassion, fairness, and kindness

Compassion

Compassion is noticing suffering, feeling concern, and being moved to help (Goetz et al., 2010): notice, feel concern, understand without judging, act. Self-compassion means treating yourself kindly when you struggle, recognizing common humanity, and holding painful feelings in balanced awareness (Neff, 2003). It is associated with lower anxiety, depression, and stress (MacBeth & Gumley, 2012). It is not self-pity. Help without humiliation: ask what is needed, offer rather than impose, and protect privacy.

Identifiable victim effect. People gave more to a single identified victim than to statistics about many (Small et al., 2007). Media framing shapes whose suffering we notice, and compassion fatigue is real. Check sources before sharing.

Fairness

PrincipleMeaningWatch out for
EqualityEveryone gets the same.Ignores different needs.
EquityRewards match contribution.Hard to measure.
NeedResources go to those who need them most.Feels unfair if not explained.

Equity tends to be preferred when productivity is the goal, equality when harmony and solidarity are, and need when caring and personal development are (Deutsch, 1975).

DimensionQuestion
DistributiveIs the outcome fair?
ProceduralWas the process fair: consistent, with voice, free of bias?
InterpersonalWas I treated with dignity and respect?
InformationalWere decisions explained honestly?

Dimensions from Colquitt (2001). A study in seven societies found children across cultures reject getting less than a peer early, while rejecting getting more appears later and varies by society (Blake et al., 2015).

Ethical dilemmas: a six-step process

  1. Identify the facts and who is affected.
  2. Name the competing values.
  3. List options, including creative ones.
  4. Consider consequences for each stakeholder.
  5. Decide and justify using fairness, care, and your values.
  6. Plan to repair any harm and to learn.

Kindness

A meta-analysis found a small to medium positive effect of performing acts of kindness on the giver's well-being (Curry et al., 2018). Kindness works best when freely chosen, fitted to the receiver's needs, and not done to be repaid. It misfires when it imposes help, publicizes the act or the person's need, burns you out, or is directed only at friends. Filipino expressions include malasakit, pakikiramay, pagdamay, paglilingkod, and bayanihan.

Observation activity rules: safety first, privacy and dignity (no photographing strangers without consent, describe behavior not individuals; Data Privacy Act, RA 10173), describe then interpret, look for the helpful as well as the harmful, and include yourself.

Pause C: whose name goes on the report?

Facilitator noteUse groups of four. Each group names the competing values, lists two options, and reports which fairness principle they used.
Scenario. You are a group leader. A groupmate who has been absent due to a family emergency asks you to put his name on the report anyway. Other members worked overtime and are angry. The teacher says all names must reflect actual contributions.
  1. What values compete?
  2. What options honor more than one of them?
  3. Who needs protection, and how would you protect their dignity?
See a possible response

Competing values: compassion for the absent member, fairness to the members who worked, honesty with the teacher.

Options: list his name with an honest description of his role, ask the teacher about an adjusted task he can complete, or acknowledge the emergency separately. Avoid pretending contributions that did not happen.

Dignity: speak to him privately, avoid shaming in the group chat, and let the angry members voice concerns in a structured way.

Part D, know: collaboration

CooperationCollaboration
PatternWork together, often by dividing a task.Create together, with joint decisions.
DecisionsOften set by one person or in advance.Shared among members.
InterdependenceLower to moderate.High.
ExampleEach member writes a section.The group designs the argument together, then writes.

Collaborative synergy means the group does better than the sum of its parts. Johnson and Johnson's (2009) five elements and Lesson 6's psychological safety and conflict skills apply here.

Diversity, collective intelligence, and roles

  • Diversity can help (wider information and perspectives) or hurt (in-group and out-group divisions), depending on the group's climate (van Knippenberg et al., 2004).
  • A study of small groups found a "collective intelligence" factor related to average social sensitivity, evenness of turn-taking, and proportion of women (Woolley et al., 2010). Replication has been mixed, so treat it as suggestive.
  • Task roles move the work forward (initiating, seeking and giving information, summarizing, evaluating). Maintenance roles keep the group working well (encouraging, harmonizing, setting norms, gatekeeping so everyone can speak). Productive groups need both (Benne & Sheats, 1948).
  • Bayanihan: the goal belongs to all and so does the work. Servant leadership: preparing the space, sharing credit, making sure the least confident member has a real part. Rotate the facilitator.
  • Team building: a meta-analysis found positive effects, strongest for activities that focused on clarifying roles and setting goals (Klein et al., 2009). Games alone are less powerful than games followed by structured discussion.
  • In the community: the Sangguniang Kabataan (RA 10742) is a formal setting where youth collaborate in governance.

Fast, but it silences the quiet member and ignores the doubter's information. This is a task role without maintenance.

See a possible response

Missing roles: maintenance roles (gatekeeping, encouraging, harmonizing) and summarizing or evaluating among task roles.

Recap

Lesson 7: what to remember

  • Interpersonal effectiveness weighs three goals: the objective, the relationship, and self-respect.
  • Say it clearly and respectfully. Read cues as hypotheses: Observe, Interpret, Check, Adjust.
  • Empathy needs self-regulation and fairness. Compassion adds the move to act.
  • Pakikipagkapwa is shared humanity. It is not the same as pakikisama. Protect amor propio and avoid undue hiya.
  • Fairness has principles and dimensions. Name which one is in play.
  • Collaboration is shared decisions plus both task and maintenance roles. Debrief after team building.
